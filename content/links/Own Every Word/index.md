description: "AI is causing \"cognitive atrophy\": people accept LLM output without understanding it, can't defend their own words, and lose the ability to spot errors. Evidence ranges from a MIT study (83% of ChatGPT writers couldn't quote a sentence they'd just produced) to a Brown economics class whose average crashed from 96 to 48 once exams went in-person, to law firms filing fabricated citations. The author's practical rules: write your own thinking before prompting, treat the struggle as the actual training, and learn what \"great\" looks like so you can judge AI output rather than blindly trusting it. The core claim is that the difficulty of doing work is what builds expertise and ownership; skip it and you get polished, forgettable, unowned output."
